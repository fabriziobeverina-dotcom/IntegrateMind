THE RETURN
VOICE-OVER SCRIPT
=================

Notes
-----
This file follows the authored scene order. Choices are shuffled for the
player during the game, but the text below keeps the original choice order.

Text in [SQUARE BRACKETS] is personalized or conditional at runtime:

[PLAYER DAYS]       Number of ceremony days
[PLAYER OPENING]    Personalized opening line
[PLAYER NAME]       Player's name
[FRIEND NAME]       Personalized friend name
[QUEUE SCENE]       Personalized queue location

The game also has short somatic prompts before some choices. They are

SCENE 00 — INTRO
=================

THE RETURN

You have come back from the jungle.
[PLAYER DAYS] days of ceremony. You are not the same.

But the world does not know this yet.
The world is still the world —
and now it is full of spirits, energies, temptations, and tests.

And a guide or two, if you have the eyes for it.

Behind the mask, you may be honest.
No self-judgement. Only what is true.

BUTTON: Enter the Return


SCENE 01 — THE AIRPORT OF SOULS
================================
DAY ONE · THE THRESHOLD

You have returned from [PLAYER OPENING].

The grey static around each person is visible now — coils of unprocessed
grief, small figures on shoulders. The man ahead carries something on his
left that you recognise. You saw it in ceremony.

A woman sits beside you, crying softly into her phone.
"Everything is fine," she says. "Everything is absolutely fine."

Something in you wants to flee — back to the jungle, back to the maloca,
back to where everything made sense.

CHOICE 1
Sit beside her in silence.

RESPONSE
You sit. You do not speak, do not offer comfort, do not perform presence.
You simply remain. After a moment the quality of her crying changes —
slightly slower, slightly less alone. She does not look at you. But
something shifted.

CHOICE 2
Look away. You are still too raw.

RESPONSE
You turn toward the window. The woman's voice continues behind you.
"Everything is fine. Everything is absolutely fine."

You think: who is she saying that for.

CHOICE 3
Open your notebook. Write before the visions fade.

RESPONSE
Your hand moves. The pen barely keeps up. Behind you the airport
continues and here, on the page, the jungle is still happening.

CHOICE 4
Close your eyes. Feel the longing to go back.

RESPONSE
Heavier than expected — a physical weight in the sternum. You do not
fight it. You let it be there, this grief for a place that is already
becoming memory.


SCENE 02 — THIRTY THOUSAND FEET
================================
DAY ONE · THE IN-BETWEEN

The plane climbs. Below, the Amazon shrinks into green geometry, then
cloud. You watch it disappear with a strange grief, as if something alive
is being slowly erased.

IF THE PLAYER LOOKED AWAY AT THE AIRPORT:
You think of the woman. Her grief is still somewhere in the world,
unwitnessed.

The man beside you — late forties, suit — glances at your jungle bracelet.
"Holiday?" he asks.

You see his soul: a bright young thing, maybe twelve, locked in a glass
cage inside his ribcage. Pressing its hands against the walls.

CHOICE 1
"Something like that." Smile. Put your headphones in.

RESPONSE
He nods, satisfied, returns to his laptop. You watch the Amazon disappear.
The music is wrong for this. You take the headphones out.

Silence. Cloud. The feeling of having kept something you did not need to
keep.

CHOICE 2
Tell him the truth. All of it.

RESPONSE
You begin. The ayahuasca. The nights in the maloca. The thing you saw on
the third ceremony that you still cannot name. He listens with the focused
expression of a man being told something he suspects might be important.

When you finish he is quiet. Then:
"My wife wants to do something like that."

You look at him. He is already looking out the window. The boy in the cage
is pressing his hands against the glass.

CHOICE 3
Ask him about his work. Listen differently — below the words.

RESPONSE
He talks for forty minutes. You follow it at two levels: the story he is
telling, and the one beneath it. At the end he says:
"I feel like I've been talking too much."

"No," you say. "You haven't."

CHOICE 4
Say nothing. Press your forehead against the cold window.

RESPONSE
The cold enters through your forehead and spreads slowly. Cloud. Then,
briefly, a dark-green geometry that could be anything.


SCENE 03 — THE KITCHEN OF YOUR PARENTS
=======================================
DAY TWO · THE FAMILIAR WORLD

Your mother has cooked. The familiar smell of home — and underneath it,
the familiar anxiety that has lived in this kitchen for forty years.

Your father asks if you ate well in Peru. He does not ask what you did
there.

Old family patterns move like smoke between the chairs. A spirit of
unspoken grief anchors itself to the corner near the window. It has been
there for decades.

Your mother refills your glass before it is empty.
"You look thin," she says.

They will never understand. You have been to the edge of the world and
back, and here you are, passing the bread.

CHOICE 1
Eat the food. Give it your full attention.

RESPONSE
You do not perform gratitude. You simply eat. The soup is too salty and
slightly overcooked and you have not tasted anything this specific in
seven days. Your mother watches you eat the way she has always watched you
eat — as if the fact of your eating is itself a small triumph.

"It's good," you say. You mean it.

CHOICE 2
Try to explain what happened.

RESPONSE
You begin. You can see, almost immediately, the moment their faces shift —
not hostile, not unkind, just the particular closing that happens when the
world is asked to expand faster than it can.

Your father nods at appropriate intervals. When you stop, he says:
"And the food? Did they feed you properly?"

You look at them. You love them. You will try again. But not tonight.

CHOICE 3
Perform the familiar role.

RESPONSE
The mask goes on before you decide to wear it. You hear yourself asking
about the neighbours, the garden, the car. You are excellent at this.

Somewhere inside, very quietly, something that came back from the jungle
observes this performance with an expression you cannot read.

CHOICE 4
Excuse yourself early. The floor of your old bedroom.

RESPONSE
Your childhood bed is too soft. You take the blanket to the floor and lie
flat, arms open, feeling the house settle around you.

You breathe. The ceremony breathes with you. This will have to be enough
for tonight.


SCENE 04 — THE QUEUE
=====================
DAY THREE · FIRST TEST
TITLE: THE [QUEUE SCENE]

The queue is long. You wait with six items. The person ahead has a
complicated transaction that is completely stalled. The people behind you
shift their weight.

Something tightens in your jaw. You notice you are already somewhere else
in your head — a story about your time, about this person you do not know.

IF THE PLAYER PERFORMED THE FAMILIAR ROLE:
At dinner you performed patience. Here, with strangers, the performance is
not available. This is what you actually are.

The person ahead is visibly mortified. The cashier's face is blank. Eight
people wait.

INTERSTITIAL — BEFORE YOU ACT
Use one of the following variants:

VARIANT 1
Place your feet flat on the floor. Feel the weight of your body.
Exhale for four slow counts.
Now — from here — choose.

VARIANT 2
Before you act: one hand on your chest. Feel it moving.
Breathe out until the breath is empty.
From this place — choose.

VARIANT 3
Your jaw is carrying something. Let it soften slightly.
Three slow breaths. Not to fix anything — just to arrive.
Present — choose.

VARIANT 4
Notice where in your body the charge is sitting.
Exhale through the mouth, long and quiet.
Make the choice from here.

IF A SOMATIC ANCHOR IS PERSONALIZED:
You recognise this — the [SOMATIC ANCHOR]. Just notice it is there.
[ONE OF THE BREATH PROMPTS ABOVE]

BUTTON: continue

CHOICE 1
Stay with the breath. Wait.

RESPONSE
The tightening in your jaw does not disappear. But you stop feeding it.
You stand there, a human being in a queue, breathing, while another human
being tries to locate money in a bag that contains everything except money.

The person behind you sighs. You feel the sigh land, and you do not join it.
Eventually the queue moves.

CHOICE 2
Step forward. Offer to cover the difference.

RESPONSE
You hear yourself say it before you decide to:
"I can cover it — please, it's fine."

The cashier looks at you. The woman looks at you.

She accepts. She cannot look at you while she accepts. In the car park she
will try to find you to pay you back. She will not be able to. This is fine.

CHOICE 3
Move to another queue.

RESPONSE
You pick up your basket and move three metres to the left. The frustration
goes nowhere in particular — it becomes part of the ambient texture of the
afternoon.

From here you can see the woman's shoulders. Still tight. Still mortified.
You look away.

CHOICE 4
Sigh. Loudly enough.

RESPONSE
The sound escapes before you fully choose it. The woman hears it — her
shoulders tighten fractionally.

A half-second of satisfaction, clean and sharp. Then the shame: you have
just used your exhale as a weapon against someone who was already having a
bad day.

You stare at the chewing gum display for the remaining minutes.


SCENE 05 — THE FRICTION
========================
DAY THREE · SECOND TEST

You are ten minutes from home. Something blocks the path — someone frozen,
oblivious, in the way.

The reaction is right there. And with it, the whole familiar architecture:
the certainty that you are right, that they are wrong, that the anger is
justified.

IF THE PLAYER HAS REACTED BEFORE:
It is the second time today the old circuitry has run. The jungle showed you
this exact wiring. You are watching it.

Behind the anger, if you look: you are tired. You want to be home. This
person is simply the nearest available explanation for that.

INTERSTITIAL — BEFORE YOU ACT
Use one of the somatic prompts from Scene 04.

CHOICE 1
Wait. Notice what is underneath.

RESPONSE
You wait. In those seconds you find, beneath the irritation: you are tired,
you have been carrying something heavy all week, and you want to be home in
a way that has nothing to do with this person.

The path clears. You go home.

CHOICE 2
Signal once. Just a notification.

RESPONSE
One brief signal. The person startles, looks up, moves. They wave a small
apology at no one in particular.

You move past them. You feel approximately nothing, which is itself a kind
of progress.

CHOICE 3
React.

RESPONSE
The reaction comes out full — more than you intended. The person startles
badly. You drive away feeling, for two seconds, justified. Then the two
seconds end.

For the rest of the drive you are explaining yourself to no one, in your
head, in a car alone.

CHOICE 4
Take a different way home entirely.

RESPONSE
You turn off before the obstruction and take the longer road — past the
canal, the way you used to go. It takes twelve extra minutes. The light on
the canal is something. You had forgotten about the canal.


SCENE 06 — THE CHILD AT THE CORNER
===================================
DAY THREE · THE ENCOUNTER

You stop at the corner shop for something small. On the step outside, a
child — maybe seven, maybe eight — is sitting alone, watching the street
with an attention that seems too still for her age.

IF THE PLAYER HAS REACTED TWICE OR MORE:
You are still carrying the residue of today. You almost walk past her. But
some small remaining thread of the ceremony slows your step.

OTHERWISE:
The day has been quiet inside you, mostly. Something about that quiet lets
you actually see her. She is very still.

As you pass, she looks directly up at you — without the usual social
hesitation of children with strangers — and says:

[CHILD WORDS]

Then she looks back at the street, as if she said nothing unusual. The
shopkeeper inside has not looked up. No one else seems to have heard.

Something stops time for a half-second. You know this feeling — the sudden
stillness of ceremony when something true is spoken into a room. The
child's eyes, in that brief moment, held something very old.

You are standing on a pavement outside a corner shop in ordinary daylight.
And something just opened.

CHOICE 1
Stop. Crouch down to her level. Say:
"You might be right. Thank you."

RESPONSE
She nods, completely unsurprised, as if she expected nothing less. Then she
looks back at the street.

You stand up. You go into the shop. You buy what you came for. On the way
out she is gone — not dramatically, just gone, the way things go in
ceremony when they are finished with you.

CHOICE 2
Smile, genuinely moved, and keep walking.

RESPONSE
The words stay with you the whole afternoon. You turn them like a stone
you found on a path: smooth on one side, rough on the other, heavier than
it looks.

CHOICE 3
Stop. Ask her name, her age, whether she is waiting for someone.

RESPONSE
She answers each question patiently, as if she has all the time there is.
Something passed between you anyway, through the ordinary exchange. She
does not explain herself further. Neither do you.

CHOICE 4
Laugh softly and go into the shop.

RESPONSE
"Kids, huh?" you say to no one in particular. The door closes. The moment
closes with it.

You buy what you came for. On the way out the step is empty.


SCENE 07 — THE CREDIT
======================
DAY FIVE · THIRD TEST

Thursday meeting. Your manager presents the proposal. The slides are yours
— built over three evenings. Your colleague nods along as if he had some
role in it. Afterward he is praised specifically. He accepts the praise
without correction.

IF THE PLAYER RECOGNISED THE GUIDE:
The child asked if what you carry is yours. This anger — is it yours? Or is
it the old story about not being seen, dressed in fresh clothes?

OTHERWISE:
The unfairness is real. And beneath it, something older: the fear that if
you do not claim what is yours, it will be taken.

After the meeting he finds you at the coffee machine and says, cheerfully:
"Great proposal by the way."

You see his energy. It is not malicious. He genuinely does not know what he
did. This does not make it acceptable. But it changes something about what
the correct response is.

INTERSTITIAL — BEFORE YOU ACT
Use one of the somatic prompts from Scene 04.

CHOICE 1
"I built that proposal. I'd appreciate it if you mentioned that to Sarah."

RESPONSE
Calm. Specific. He looks at you for a moment with an expression you cannot
entirely read. Then:
"Oh. Yeah. Of course. I didn't mean to — I'll talk to her."

You do not know if he will. But you said the thing. The thing has been said.

CHOICE 2
Say nothing here. Book time with the manager directly.

RESPONSE
"Thanks," you say, and mean nothing by it. You take out your phone and
send your manager a message asking for fifteen minutes.

She responds immediately: tomorrow at 3. You put your phone away. The
anger has somewhere to go now. It becomes something more like preparation.

CHOICE 3
"Thanks." Let it go.

RESPONSE
He smiles back, satisfied, and returns to the office. The anger does not
leave. It has nowhere to go, so it finds a place to live — somewhere between
your shoulder blades, low and persistent. It will be there at dinner. It
will be there tomorrow.

CHOICE 4
"I know. I wrote it."

RESPONSE
He holds the air between you for a moment — a fraction longer than is
comfortable — then nods once and walks back down the corridor.

Nothing has been resolved. But something has been placed correctly, and you
feel its weight settle into its proper position.


SCENE 08 — THE MIRROR
======================
DAY FOUR · THE MIRROR
TITLE: [FRIEND NAME]

[FRIEND NAME] has known you since you were seventeen. Two drinks in,
telling a story about work. [FRIEND NAME] asks what the jungle was like.
"Intense?" they offer, smiling.

IF THE PLAYER PERFORMED THE FAMILIAR ROLE:
You did it again at dinner. You notice yourself checking: is it happening
now too?

IF THE PLAYER RECOGNISED THE GUIDE:
The child's question is still somewhere in you. What are you carrying into
this room, into this friendship?

Two people: the friend you love, and a ghost version of you — the one you
might have become. The ghost sits comfortably in the chair. It orders
another round. It is not unhappy.

"You seem different," [FRIEND NAME] says. Not a compliment, not a criticism.
An observation, delivered with slight wariness.

CHOICE 1
"I am different. I don't fully know how yet."

RESPONSE
You meet their eyes. You do not explain further. [FRIEND NAME] seems to
consider pressing — and then decides not to.
"Well," they say. "You seem good."

It is not quite right, but it is kind. You let it be kind.

CHOICE 2
Deflect. Become whoever the room needs.

RESPONSE
You pull their story toward you and inhabit it perfectly. [FRIEND NAME]
relaxes into the conversation like a person taking off a coat.

On the way home you will not be entirely sure what you said. The evening
will feel both successful and slightly hollow.

CHOICE 3
Order water. Your body does not want the drink.

RESPONSE
You push the glass aside. [FRIEND NAME] raises an eyebrow.
"Still detoxing?"

"Something like that." At some point you notice you are more present than
usual. [FRIEND NAME] does not comment. But they lean forward more than they
usually do.

CHOICE 4
Excuse yourself. Breathe through it alone.

RESPONSE
The thing — grief, irritation, you cannot tell — moves through you in
ninety seconds. Then it is gone. You rinse your face. You go back out.
[FRIEND NAME] is looking at their phone and does not look up as you sit
down, which is exactly what you needed.


SCENE 09 — THE LAUNDROMAT OF THE SOUL
======================================
DAY FIVE · EVENING · THE LAUNDROMAT

You needed to do laundry. This is not symbolic. The laundry genuinely
needed doing.

The laundromat is open until midnight. It is 10pm. You are the only one
here.

You load the machine. In go the clothes from the week. The drum begins to
turn.

You sit down on the plastic bench to wait. And then, without any particular
warning, you notice what is in the machine.

It is not the clothes.

It is your heart. Turning slowly, pressed against the porthole glass,
trailing its valves. Then your lungs — both of them, enormous and grey-pink,
folding and unfolding in the warm water. Your liver, your kidneys, your
intestines coiling in the suds. Your throat — the one that has never spoken
as clearly as you needed it to — spiralling in the foam like something
dredged from deep water.

You look around. There is no one else here. The vending machine hums.

The machine runs its full cycle. The water keeps changing colour.
Eventually it runs clear. The machine beeps. The drum slows. You open the
door.

One of them is shining. The [SHINING ORGAN]:
[ORGAN DESCRIPTION]

[ORGAN REASON]

You put everything back carefully, without haste. You move the load to the
dryer. You sit back down.

On your phone: a review for this laundromat.
Four stars. "Changed my life." — M. Fontana, fourteen months ago.

You type yours:
Five stars. "The machines are reliable. I would say more but I am not sure
I have the language yet."

You post it.

You wait for the dryer. The light in here is very steady and very ordinary
and you are, for no reason you can fully defend, completely fine.

ORGAN VARIANTS
--------------

THE EYES
You hold them up under the fluorescent light. They are clear in a way eyes
are not usually clear — the way glass is clear after rain. You see through
them into the back of the room, into the parking lot, and further, further.
You blink. The world blinks back.

Why:
You saw what was there to be seen. The guide, the child, the shape of
things beneath the shape of things.

THE THROAT
It is warm and slightly luminous and smells of mapacho and river. You hold
it up. Inside it, like a bell that has just been struck, something is still
vibrating gently. The sound it never made is still in there, circling. Now
it has been washed, maybe it can.

Why:
You spoke at least once when you could have swallowed it. The throat
remembers.

THE JAW
You almost do not recognise it. You have held it so tight for so long —
this week, and before this week, and the whole life before this week — that
it barely knows what loose feels like. But here, clean, warm from the dryer,
it is simply a jaw. It unclenches by itself in your hands.

Why:
You caught the tightening. More than once. You did not act from it.

THE LUNGS
They are larger than you expected and very light and when you hold them up
they catch the fluorescent light in a way that suggests they have been
waiting, for some time, to be given this much air. You breathe in. They
expand. The ceremony is still in there, the icaros, the fire, the dark
before the dark breaks.

Why:
You came back to the breath. Again and again this week, you came back.

THE HEART
It is exactly the size of a fist and it is doing what hearts do, steadily,
without drama. But under the light it is different — brighter, or more
specific, as if the washing has removed something that was dulling it. You
hold it and feel it beat against your palm and you think: this.

Why:
You went back. The ceremony found you in the night and you went back.

THE STOMACH
You are not sure what you expected, but not this: warm, quiet, a little
embarrassed, like something that has been working very hard without
acknowledgement for a long time. You hold it and say, quietly:
I know. I know.

It seems like the right thing to say.

Why:
It held everything this week that had nowhere else to go. It held it
without complaint.

CHOICE 1
Hold your heart carefully, like something that has survived a long
crossing.

RESPONSE
It is warm. Heavier than expected. You hold it for a moment before placing
it back. It slots in with a small definitive click, like a key finding its
lock. You sit back down. The dryer turns. The city continues its business,
entirely unaware that something just happened in here.

CHOICE 2
Hold your throat up to the light. Try to see through it.

RESPONSE
It is translucent in a way you did not expect. You can see the shape of
things that passed through it and things that did not — words spoken clearly
and ones that went back down. You study it for a long moment. Then you put
it back gently, like a correction that has finally been made.

CHOICE 3
Laugh. Because this is genuinely funny.

RESPONSE
You start laughing — the involuntary kind, from behind the sternum, which
has just been washed and is still slightly damp. The fluorescent light does
not react. The vending machine does not react. You laugh until your eyes
water, sitting alone in the laundromat at 10pm holding your own clean
organs.

When you stop, you feel lighter than you have in a week. You put everything
back. The dryer turns.

CHOICE 4
Sit quietly and hold each one in turn.

RESPONSE
Heart, lungs, liver, throat, the stomach that has been clenching since
Tuesday. You hold each one for a moment — not trying to fix anything, just
letting it know you are paying attention. When they are all back in place,
you feel different. Not healed. Not resolved. Just attended to. Which turns
out to be something.


SCENE 10 — THE SPIRIT OF THE OLD PATTERN
=========================================
DAY SIX · THE DESCENT

11pm. Alone. The pull: [TEMPTATION NAME]. [TEMPTATION PULL]

IF THE PLAYER HAS REACTED:
The reactions this week were real. The pattern knows where the cracks are.
It has been watching.

IF THE PLAYER RECOGNISED THE GUIDE:
The child asked what you are carrying. This pull — how long have you been
carrying it? Did you choose it, or did it choose you?

It stands in the corner. Not monstrous — beautiful, almost. Ancient,
patient eyes. In ceremony you saw it, named it, wept with it. It has waited
patiently for your return.

"You cannot sustain this," it says quietly.
"You know what you are. Come back."

INTERSTITIAL — BEFORE YOU ACT
Use one of the somatic prompts from Scene 04.

CHOICE 1
Say its name out loud. Sit with it.

RESPONSE
You say the name in the empty room. It sounds smaller than it feels. You
sit with it the way you sat with things in ceremony: without trying to
change them.

The pull does not leave. But it loses a dimension. It becomes a thing you
are watching. You sit there for a while. Then you make tea.

CHOICE 2
Write it. The pull, the shape, what it promises.

RESPONSE
You open the journal. You write: "it is here again." Then what it feels
like, what it offers, what you know about its cost. The writing does not
make it disappear. But it makes it legible.

You write for twenty minutes. At the end you draw a thin line under the
last sentence. For tonight, you do not act.

CHOICE 3
Give in. Partially. Enough to take the edge off.

RESPONSE
You tell yourself this is also integration. These are true things. They are
also the things the pattern has learned to whisper to sound reasonable.

Afterward, the edge is off. Something else has taken its place — a thin,
familiar emptiness you know how to live with, because you have been living
with it for years.

CHOICE 4
Call someone.

RESPONSE
A friend who picks up late, who does not ask why, who simply begins talking
about their week. Their voice fills the room. The pull retreats to the edge
where it waits, patient, for another opening.

You talk for half an hour. You do not mention what you were about to do.
You do not need to.


SCENE 11 — WHAT THE DARK ASKS
==============================
DAY SIX · [CLOCK] · THE CEILING

[CLOCK]. The room is dark. You are awake staring at the ceiling. This is
the part nobody puts in the retreat brochures.

IF THE PLAYER GAVE IN:
The giving-in has not helped. The emptiness it left is louder than the
craving was.

IF THE PLAYER SAT WITH OR WROTE ABOUT THE PATTERN:
You held last night. Something noticed. The room has a slightly different
quality.

The ceiling is not blank. You can still see — faintly — the serpent.
Patient as water. It traces the same line across the plaster, and then it
turns its head.

A single eye, the colour of jungle canopy, finds yours. Something like an
invitation moves through the dark of the room.

IF THE PLAYER RECOGNISED THE GUIDE:
The child. The ceremony. The serpent now. The same current, different forms.
Something is trying to reach you through every available opening.

CHOICE 1
Close your eyes. Follow the serpent back.

RESPONSE
You close your eyes. You release the room — the ceiling, the clock, the
weight of the week. The serpent moves. You move with it. The distance
between here and the maloca folds, the way water folds around a stone, and —

CHOICE 2
Lie still. Let the dark ask.

RESPONSE
You lie there. The dark asks. You do not answer. This is its own kind of
conversation — the kind where staying in the room together is the point.

At some point the quality of the darkness changes. Dawn is arriving
somewhere else first.

CHOICE 3
Get up. Make tea. Write until the light comes.

RESPONSE
The kitchen is cold. Standing in it in the dark at this hour feels —
briefly, strangely — like exactly the right place to be.

You write until the window begins to separate from the wall. Three pages.
You do not know what they say yet. That is for later.

CHOICE 4
Body scan. Feet upward. Let sleep find you.

RESPONSE
Feet. Ankles. Calves. The knee that always carries more than it admits.
The hips. The belly. The chest —

You do not reach the chest.


SCENE 12 — THE JUNGLE FINDS YOU IN YOUR BED
============================================
NIGHT · THE RETURN WITHIN THE RETURN

The ceiling dissolves. The serpent folds the distance between your room and
the maloca into nothing, the way water folds around a stone.

You are in the ceremony. The fire is still burning. The icaros still move
through the air. [SHAMAN TEXT]

And then your animal guide steps from the darkness at the edge of the
firelight.

[ANIMAL DESCRIPTION]

In the guide's gaze you see the week laid out — not as memory, but as
landscape. You see the patterns from outside: where you contracted, where
something moved through freely.

Then the guide moves toward you and places something against your chest.
Not a paw, not a wing. Something that has no shape in ordinary language.
But you feel it land. And you recognise it — it is the thing you almost
discarded this week. The guide gives it back as if it is the most important
object in the room.

IF THE PLAYER RECOGNISED THE GUIDE:
The guide shows you one more thing: the child on the step. These openings
come in every form. The question is only whether you are moving fast enough
to miss them.

OTHERWISE:
The guide shows you other moments along the map of your week — possible
openings. Not accusation. Just information.

CHOICE 1
Ask the guide what it placed in your chest.

RESPONSE
It looks at you for a long moment. Then it shows you: it is your own
attention. The full, undefended kind. You had been rationing it all week.

PRIMARY ARCHETYPE — THE SOVEREIGN RETURNING
--------------------------------------------
You are The Sovereign Returning — you crossed the threshold without handing
your authority to the experience. You received what came and brought it
back into the body, where insight becomes a way of moving rather than a
story you tell about yourself. The world may still be difficult, but you
are no longer waiting for difficulty to tell you who you are. Your work now
is to govern this gift with humility, attention, and ordinary acts of care.

PRIMARY ARCHETYPE — THE JAGUAR-TOUCHED
--------------------------------------
You are The Jaguar-Touched — something in the dark recognized you, and you
had the courage to go back when it called. The encounter did not make you
special; it made you responsible for what you saw. You carry a charge that
cannot be explained away by the return to ordinary life. Let the jaguar
remain wild in you, but give its knowing a human rhythm: meals, messages,
rest, and the next honest choice.

PRIMARY ARCHETYPE — THE EMBODIED WITNESS
----------------------------------------
You are The Embodied Witness — you learned to let the invisible register
as sensation, breath, weight, and action. Your mind can name what is
happening, but your deeper gift is staying present while the body tells the
truth. You do not need to force transformation when attention is already
changing the shape of the moment. Keep returning from explanation to
contact, where the medicine can become lived knowledge.

PRIMARY ARCHETYPE — THE CARTOGRAPHER
------------------------------------
You are The Cartographer — you processed this crossing by mapping it,
naming it, and building a language for what you saw. The mind was your
instrument and, at times, your refuge; its patterns helped you find a route
through uncertainty. A map is a form of devotion when it serves contact
rather than replacing it. Keep drawing, but remember that the territory
asks to be inhabited as much as understood.

PRIMARY ARCHETYPE — THE OPEN GATE
---------------------------------
You are The Open Gate — you recognized that guidance rarely arrives wearing
the costume you expected. A child, a stranger, or an ordinary sentence
opened a space in your attention, and you chose not to rush past it. Your
receptivity is not passivity; it is the willingness to let the world
interrupt the plan. Stay close to the small doors, because the path keeps
appearing in forms that do not announce themselves.

PRIMARY ARCHETYPE — THE WOUNDED HEALER
--------------------------------------
You are The Wounded Healer — you met the places where your own tenderness
and your own unfinished work touch. What you know is not abstract: it has a
pulse, a history, and a body that has had to carry it. This gives you a
particular kind of compassion, but it also asks you not to turn
understanding into self-abandonment. Let care move in both directions,
toward others and back toward the person doing the caring.

PRIMARY ARCHETYPE — THE BRIDGE WALKER
--------------------------------------
You are The Bridge Walker — you can carry the enchanted and the ordinary
without demanding that either one cancel the other. You know how strange a
luminous insight can feel under fluorescent light, and you keep walking
anyway. Your gift is translation: bringing what matters across without
flattening it. The bridge is made from repeated crossings, not a single
perfect arrival.

PRIMARY ARCHETYPE — THE ONE STILL CROSSING
------------------------------------------
You are The One Still Crossing — the river has not narrowed into an answer,
and you are honest enough not to pretend that it has. Much is moving
beneath the surface even when the visible changes are small. This middle
place is not a failure of integration; it is where integration is made.
Keep your attention near the next step, because the crossing does not
require you to see the far bank yet.
